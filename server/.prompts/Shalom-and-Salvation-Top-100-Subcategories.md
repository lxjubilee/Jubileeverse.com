## #1 — What is Shalom? Beyond "Peace" — 98/100 [Foundational]

**Paleo-Hebrew Analysis:** Shin-Lamed-Vav-Mem: "The fire guided by the shepherd, nailed over chaos." Shalom is not the absence of conflict — it is the consuming fire of Yahuah, directed by the Shepherd's staff, permanently fastened over the waters of chaos. Shalom is divine order imposed on disorder.

The Western world has reduced shalom to "peace" — a passive, conflict-free state. But the Hebrew shalom means completeness, wholeness, nothing broken, nothing missing, nothing lacking. This subcategory redefines shalom for the JubileeVerse audience as the comprehensive state of being that Yahuah designed from the beginning. The evangelist does not offer "peace of mind." The evangelist offers shalom — total restoration of every dimension of life: spiritual, physical, relational, emotional, financial, and eternal. This is the evangelist's opening declaration to a fractured world.

## #2 — Yeshua: The Door of Salvation (Delet HaYeshuah) — 98/100 [Foundational]

**Paleo-Hebrew Analysis:** Yeshua: Yod-Shin-Vav-Ayin — "The hand that destroys is nailed and sees." Salvation is the hand of Yahuah destroying what enslaves, nailing it permanently, and seeing/knowing the one who is freed. Yeshua's name IS the gospel.

Yeshua did not just bring salvation — His name means salvation. Yod-Shin-Vav-Ayin pictographically tells the entire story of the gospel in four letters. This subcategory establishes Yeshua as the singular doorway to salvation and shalom, exploring the meaning of His name through Paleo-Hebrew, His titles (Mashiach, Immanuel, Lamb of Yahuah, Lion of Yehudah), and His declaration: "I am the door. Whoever enters through Me will be saved." The evangelist's entire message is contained in one name.

## #3 — The Gospel of Shalom (Besorah HaShalom) — 97/100 [Foundational]

**Paleo-Hebrew Analysis:** Besorah: Bet-Sin-Resh-Hey — "The house of the thorn leading, revealed." The good news is the revelation that the house of thorns (suffering) has been led to its purpose — redemption is revealed through the wound.

The gospel is not merely "believe and go to heaven." The besorah (good news) is the announcement that shalom — total wholeness — is available now through Yeshua HaMashiach. This subcategory reclaims the fullness of the gospel message from its Western reduction to fire insurance and restores it to the Hebraic declaration of Yeshayahu 52:7: "How beautiful upon the mountains are the feet of him who brings besorah, who publishes shalom." The evangelist publishes shalom — announces it, proclaims it, makes it public.

## #4 — Born Again: The Moment Everything Changes — 97/100 [Foundational]

**Paleo-Hebrew Analysis:** Yalad: Yod-Lamed-Dalet — "The hand of the shepherd at the door." Birth is the shepherd's hand opening the door. Being born again is the divine shepherd opening a door that was never open before.

Yeshua told Nicodemus: "Unless one is born again, he cannot see the Kingdom of Elohim" (Yochanan 3:3). This subcategory explores the moment of spiritual rebirth — what happens in the spiritual realm when a person crosses from death to life. It is not reformation; it is regeneration. Not improvement; but new creation. The evangelist carries the midwife anointing — assisting in spiritual birth, cutting the cord of the old life, and welcoming the newborn into the family of Yahuah.

**Paleo-Hebrew Analysis:** Yeshua slept in the storm, then spoke to it. Shalom is not dependent on the storm stopping — it is the authority to sleep while the storm rages and the power to command it when the time comes.

Philippians 4:7 — "The shalom of Elohim, which surpasses all understanding, will guard your hearts and minds in Mashiach Yeshua." This subcategory explores the supernatural dimension of shalom — a peace that does not make sense to the rational mind because it exists independent of circumstances. The evangelist offers this shalom to a world in perpetual anxiety, declaring that there is a peace available that the world cannot give and cannot take away.

## #6 — The Cross: Where Justice and Mercy Meet — 96/100 [Foundational]

**Paleo-Hebrew Analysis:** The cross is where the vertical (Yahuah's justice descending) meets the horizontal (Yahuah's mercy extending). At the intersection point, blood was shed and shalom was purchased.

The cross of Yeshua is the epicenter of salvation — the place where every demand of justice was satisfied and every expression of mercy was released simultaneously. This subcategory explores the theology of the cross through Hebraic lenses: the Passover lamb fulfillment, the Yom Kippur sacrifice, the Avrahamic covenant completion, and the prophetic fulfillment of Yeshayahu 53.
