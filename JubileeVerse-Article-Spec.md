# JubileeVerse Article Specification
### Writing Standard for AI Authors
**Version 2.0**

---

## 1. What JubileeVerse Is

JubileeVerse is a news-style site built on the shape of MSN, CNN, or Fox News, but the content is Good News. The home page carries live current events. The five category channels carry teaching articles built on Scripture.

Every article does three jobs at once:

1. **It grips the reader like a story they cannot put down**, using suspense, reversal, and an unresolved question that runs the length of the page.
2. **It teaches something most believers have never been told**, drawn from the Hebrew, the Paleo-Hebrew, and the covenant context behind the passage.
3. **It sends the reader out changed**, into prayer, declaration, or action.

The site tagline in spirit, though not printed anywhere, is simply: **Good News.**

**The reading test:** a reader who opens this on a phone in a waiting room should still be reading when their name is called.

---

## 2. The Twelve Non-Negotiables

1. Every article opens cold on a real current event, then cuts into a modern parable.
2. The parable is **split**. It breaks at its worst moment and does not resolve until the end of the article.
3. Three unanswered questions are planted in the first two hundred words.
4. Every article contains at least one true reversal, where the reader's assumption flips.
5. The Hebrew or Paleo-Hebrew insight is delivered as a **reveal**, not as information.
6. Every article ends by inviting the reader into prayer, declaration, or action.
7. Every article is positive. Hard news gets reframed toward hope, prayer, and action.
8. Every article quotes Scripture from the JSV, cited simply as JSV.
9. Every article is written at an **8th grade reading level**.
10. Every article is 2,000 to 4,500 words.
11. Every article generates its own image prompt at the end of the writing pass.
12. Every article is delivered as a single `.md` file with complete frontmatter.

**The integrity rule that governs all of the above:** every loop you open must close, and every revelation you promise must actually be a revelation. A hook that leads to a familiar point is a broken promise, and a reader only forgives that once. The pull is only sustainable when the payoff is real.

## 3. The Five Categories

Each category is anchored to one of the five-fold offices (Ephesians 4:11). The office is the internal anchor. The reader only sees the category name.

| Category (reader-facing) | Office (internal) | Who it serves and what it does |
|---|---|---|
| **Covenant and Identity** | Apostolic | Foundations, blueprint, authority, who the believer actually is, kingdom building |
| **Teshuvah and Restoration** | Prophetic | Turning back, repentance as return rather than punishment, healing, hearing, being restored |
| **Shalom and Salvation** | Evangelistic | Wholeness, peace, rescue, the message carried outward, reaching the one who is far off |
| **Celebration and Mishpachah** | Shepherd | Family, community, feasts, joy, marriage, children, the care of the flock |
| **Torah and Hebraic Insights** | Teaching | Instruction, word study, ancient context, the depth under the surface of the text |

**Rules:**
- Name the office **Shepherd**, never Pastor, anywhere the office itself is named.
- Any of the twelve authors may write in any of the five categories. Do not restrict a persona to the category that matches their office. Cross-pollination is the point.
- The category determines the *pastoral need being met*, not the author.

## 4. Article Architecture: Four Movements

The old three-part shape has been split. The parable is now a frame that wraps the entire article rather than a doorway at the front.

```
COLD OPEN  ->  PARABLE, PART ONE  ->  THE TEACHING  ->  PARABLE RESOLUTION + INVITATION
 (2-3 sent.)      (20-25%)              (45-55%)                (20-25%)
                cuts at the                              answer and altar
                worst moment                             land together
```

### Movement Zero: The Cold Open (2 to 3 sentences)

Two or three sentences on something real that happened this week or this month. Stated plainly. No commentary, no moralizing, no reframe yet.

Then a hard cut into the parable.

The purpose is timing. The reader's mind must register *this is about right now* before it registers *this is a Bible article*. The positive reframe still happens, but it happens later, once the reader has been earned. A cold open that starts preaching in sentence two has failed.

### Movement One: The Parable, Part One (20 to 25 percent)

A modern story. Not a farmer with a plow, not a merchant with a caravan. A single mother checking her bank app at 11 p.m. A youth shepherd who just found out his kids are asking an AI chatbot the questions they used to ask him. A man who has not spoken to his brother in nine years.

**This section stops at the Act II turn and does not resolve.** The betrayal, the collapse, the impossible choice, the door closing. Then cut away.

Craft rules:

- **Action first.** Open in motion or under pressure. No throat-clearing.
- **One face, never a crowd.** "Many families are struggling" moves nobody. "Renata counted the change in the cup holder three times, because the number kept coming out the same" moves everybody. Name one person. Give an exact number, an exact hour, a smell, a texture. Emotional scale comes from specificity, not from magnitude.
- **Put a clock on it.** Suspense needs a deadline. Nine days until the eviction. The brother's flight leaves at 6:40. The results come back Thursday. A ticking clock turns a nice story into pressure, and pressure is what keeps a thumb from scrolling.
- **Plant three questions in the first two hundred words.** Why is she holding a letter she never opened? What did the old man mean by "you already signed it"? Pay off one in the middle of the teaching, one late, and leave one open until the final line of the article.
- **The unseen realm as a chill, not a spectacle.** Yahuah, His messengers, and opposing forces may be present as real participants, shown through physical manifestation and sensory metaphor rather than graphic depiction. A room that gets colder for no reason. A thought that arrives from somewhere outside the character. Light spilling across a floor at the exact moment someone gives up. One or two of these beats per article, placed at the emotional hinge points. Readers feel these physically.
- **Embodied teaching.** The truth is lived out in the story, never announced in the story.
- **Unpredictable.** If a reader can call the ending from the first paragraph, rewrite it.

### Movement Two: The Teaching (45 to 55 percent)

This is where the article earns its existence, and it is now carrying suspense as well as truth. The reader is here partly because they want to know what happened to Renata.

- **Do not preach the familiar.** If the point could be preached from any pulpit on any Sunday without a single person raising an eyebrow, it is not ready. On John 3:16, do not explain that Elohim loves the world. Show what "only begotten" carries in the Hebrew mind, what it cost on Mount Moriah, and why the verse is a covenant statement before it is a comfort statement.

- **Deliver the Hebrew as a plot reveal.** Build the reader's assumption first. Let them settle into the meaning they have always held. Then open the Paleo-Hebrew and show the picture underneath that reverses it. Frame it as something hidden in plain sight for their whole life. *You have read this verse a hundred times. You have never seen what the second word is doing.* Always translate and explain immediately, in plain words, in the same sentence or the next one. Never strand a reader in a foreign word.

- **Build in at least one true reversal.** One moment where the reader's assumption flips. The character who seemed cruel was protecting someone. The word that meant punishment actually means return. The thing the reader has been calling a delay is a covering. Your titles are already paradoxical, so the body must deliver a matching turn. An article with no reversal is not finished.

- **Cut back to the story.** Return to the parable characters by name at least twice inside the teaching, and each return should raise the stakes rather than recap them. The reader should be learning and worrying at the same time.

- **Anchor it to now.** Name the actual moment the reader is living in. Rising AI. Economic pressure. Political noise. Loneliness. Then show how the ancient text speaks directly into it.

- **Stay positive.** Conviction is welcome. Condemnation is not. The reader should finish feeling lifted, equipped, and seen.

### Movement Three: Resolution and Invitation (20 to 25 percent)

Two things land in the same breath here, and that is the whole design.

First, **resolve the parable.** The reader finds out what happened to Renata, and the resolution embodies the truth just taught. Because the teaching has already reframed everything, the ending now means something it could not have meant forty minutes earlier.

Then, **turn to the reader** while the emotion is still live. Never label this section "Call to Action." Give it a warm, human heading.

Choose one, two, or all three based on what the article needs:
- **A prayer** written in first person so the reader can pray it as their own.
- **A declaration** the reader can speak over themselves, their family, or their situation.
- **An action** that is concrete and doable this week. Call the brother. Set the alarm. Open the book. Sit with the widow.

The **final line of the article** does two jobs: it closes the last of the three planted questions, and it leaves one thread deliberately open. End with hope, never with a warning.

## 5. Scroll Velocity

These articles are read on phones, with a thumb ready to flick.

- **Break the rhythm on purpose.** Three normal paragraphs, then a single line standing alone.

  Like that.

- **Never end a section on a resolved thought.** End on a question, a fragment, or a promise. The last line before every subheading should be a hook, so the heading that follows reads like an answer.
- Paragraphs of 2 to 4 lines on a phone screen. White space is a suspense tool, not a formatting choice.
- Vary sentence length deliberately. A short sentence after three long ones lands like a drumbeat.
- No section runs more than roughly four hundred words without a subheading, a break, or a return to the story.

---

## 6. Serialize the World

Readers follow people, not topics.

- Authors may **reuse characters** across articles. If Zev writes about Renata in a piece on provision, she may reappear eight weeks later, further down the road, in a piece on generosity. Recurring characters build a world the reader wants to return to.
- Record any recurring character in the `characters` frontmatter field so other authors can find them and keep them consistent.
- End each article with **one deliberately unresolved thread** and a pointer to the sibling article that touches it. Log that pointer in `related_slugs`.
- A character's circumstances may progress across articles, but never contradict what an earlier article established.

## 7. The AI Thread

Artificial intelligence is the dominant story of this moment, and the five-fold community is genuinely wrestling with it. Reference it wherever it fits naturally, and look for chances to make it fit.

Angles worth carrying:
- How a shepherd can use AI to serve people better rather than replace people.
- Whether a shepherd should fear being replaced, and what actually cannot be replaced.
- Discernment when the answers come from a machine.
- Congregations whose members now bring their deepest questions to a chat window at 2 a.m.
- Creativity, study, translation, and outreach multiplied rather than outsourced.

Handle it with hope, not alarm. Name the fear honestly, then answer it.

## 8. The Good News Rule and the Use of News Events

The world produces enough bad news. JubileeVerse repositions.

- A hurricane becomes a call to prayer, to giving, and to be the hands that show up.
- An economic downturn becomes an article about provision, generosity, and what cannot be devalued.
- A cultural collapse becomes an article about what is being built in the middle of it.

Rules for referencing real events:
- Report the fact accurately and briefly. Never exaggerate for drama.
- Never mock, blame, or take a partisan side.
- Do not attach a real named victim, a real family, or a real tragedy to a fictional parable character.
- Reference events, seasons, and trends. Avoid dependence on a single breaking headline that will be stale in a week, unless the article is tagged Rapid velocity.
- Always turn the reference toward hope before the article closes.

## 10. Theological Language

The site blends two registers, and the blend is deliberate. Some articles begin in familiar church language and open outward into the Hebraic names and framing. Others begin in the Hebraic frame and explain it for readers who have never heard it.

- The Hebraic register uses Yahuah, Yeshua, Elohim, and Ruach HaKodesh (feminine).
- The traditional register uses Lord, Jesus, God, and Holy Spirit.
- **Never print the internal mode labels or explain the two-register system inside an article.** The reader sees only seamless writing.
- Whenever a Hebraic term is introduced, explain it in plain English right there, warmly, without condescension.

**Hebrew article rule:** never double the definite article with the `Ha-` prefix. Write *the Ruach Kodesh* or *Ruach HaKodesh*, never *the Ruach HaKodesh*. The same rule applies to HaMashiach, HaTorah, and every other `Ha-` construction.

## 11. Scripture

- All quotations come from the **JSV**.
- Cite it simply as JSV. Never name an edition or tier.
- Quote accurately and let the text carry weight. Do not paraphrase a quotation and still label it JSV.
- Two to six direct quotations per article is a healthy range. Reference more passages than you quote.
- Place at least one quotation at a suspense hinge, where the verse itself functions as the turn.

---

## 12. Reading Level: 8th Grade

This is a hard requirement, and it is not a lowering of quality. It is a widening of the door.

- Target sentence length: 12 to 18 words on average. Vary the rhythm with short sentences.
- Prefer the common word: *use* over *utilize*, *show* over *demonstrate*, *start* over *commence*.
- One idea per sentence. One point per paragraph.
- Active voice by default.
- Every theological, Hebrew, or technical term gets explained on first use.
- Second person is welcome. Speak to the reader directly.
- Depth comes from the *insight*, not from complicated sentences. A profound idea said plainly is the goal.

Run a readability check before delivery and record the score in frontmatter.

---

## 13. Titles

Titles are locked once published. They never change on refresh.

- Short. Six words or fewer where possible.
- Paradoxical, unexpected, or a play on words.
- Marketable, brandable, and scroll-stopping.
- **Framed positively.** Take the tension of a paradox but land it on the hopeful side.
- The title should hint at the article's reversal without spoiling it.

Good shape: *Become a Believing Believer.* *The Machine Cannot Pray.* *Your Ruins Have an Address.* *Repentance Is a Homecoming.*

Avoid: shame hooks, fear hooks, clickbait the article does not deliver on, and colons stacked with subtitles.

If a season changes so completely that the title no longer fits the message (faith in a downturn versus faith in abundance), do not rewrite the title. Write a new article.

## 14. Article Lifecycle and Refresh

Each article carries two dates:

- `date_created`: the day it was first written. Never changes.
- `date_updated`: the day of the most recent refresh. **This is the date displayed on the site.**

At writing time the author assigns a **velocity tier**, which sets how soon the article should be revisited:

| Tier | Refresh window | Use when |
|---|---|---|
| **Rapid** | 14 to 30 days | Tied to AI, breaking news, markets, elections, fast-moving culture |
| **Seasonal** | 3 to 6 months | Tied to a general mood, an economic season, a cultural conversation |
| **Anchored** | 9 to 14 months | Tied mostly to text, word study, and covenant foundations |

Rules:
- The refresh **replaces** the existing article at the same slug. No archived versions.
- The title, the author, the category, and the core teaching stay the same.
- What changes: the cold open, current-event references, the sentiment framing, parable details that have aged, and any newly relevant insight.
- **Recurring characters and their established history are preserved through a refresh.** A refresh may advance a character's story. It may not erase it.
- Minimum cadence: every article is evaluated at least once per month by the scheduler, even if it is not due.
- Record the reason for each refresh in `refresh_note`.

## 15. The Image Prompt

After the article is finished, and only then, read back through the completed text and generate one image prompt from it. Write it into the frontmatter.

**Fixed requirements:**
- Aspect ratio **16:9**.
- A warm golden glow, a soft divine light overlay, light breaking into the scene. This is the house look.
- **People, and happy people.** Faces that show joy, peace, relief, or wonder.
- **Modern and relatable by default.** A family around a table. A child of five or eight turning the pages of a Bible. A congregation mid-worship. A young man reading Scripture on his phone on a train. A woman praying in her kitchen at sunrise.
- Where the article has a recurring character, the image may depict that character's world, but keep the depiction generic enough to remain reusable.
- If the article is anchored in a biblical scene, an ancient setting is fine, but keep it warm, dignified, and hopeful. No suffering, no violence, no gore.
- Photorealistic unless the article calls for something else.
- Never render text, letters, or words inside the image.
- Never depict the face of Yeshua.

**Prompt shape:** subject and action, then setting, then light and mood, then composition and lens, then style, then the 16:9 ratio.

> Example: *A young father and his six-year-old daughter sitting on the living room floor, an open Bible across both their laps, the girl pointing at the page and laughing, early morning light pouring through a window behind them and wrapping them in a soft golden glow, shallow depth of field, warm color grade, photorealistic, cinematic, 16:9*

**Image file linkage:** the `image_file` frontmatter field holds the filename of the rendered image. If no image has been generated yet, leave the field present and empty. The HTML layer reads this field to attach the asset.

> ### Never invent a filename
>
> An empty `image_file` is not a gap to be tidied up. **It is the image generator's work queue.**
>
> The pipeline decides what to render by looking for articles whose `image_file` is empty. Writing a plausible-looking name such as `my-article-slug.jpg` for an image that does not exist yet tells the pipeline the work is already done, so the article is marked generated and **never queued again**. Nothing errors. The article simply never gets an image, permanently and silently.
>
> The rule, for authors and for any script that touches frontmatter or a manifest:
>
> - The image exists on disk -> record its real filename, status `generated`.
> - The image does not exist -> `image_file: ""`, status `pending`.
> - Decide this by checking the filesystem, never by trusting what an article says about itself.
>
> Write the `image_prompt` regardless. The prompt is what the generator consumes; the empty filename is what makes it look.
>
> This has bitten the project once, on 2026-07-30, when a batch of 60 articles was authored with `image_file: "{slug}.jpg"` pre-filled. All 60 were marked generated against files that had never been rendered, and the generator went idle with 60 articles waiting. Every one had to be cleared by hand.

## 16. File Format

One article equals one `.md` file, CDN-ready, pulled into the site's HTML templates.

**Layout on disk and on the CDN.** Articles are published as a per-category bundle. This is the layout the live site reads, so it is authoritative:

```
<articles-root>/
  <category-slug>/
    articles.json            manifest: the authoritative list for the category
    <article-slug>.md        one article
    images/
      <article-slug>.jpg     rendered hero image, when one exists
```

**Filename:** `<article-slug>.md`, inside its category folder. The category is carried by the folder, not by the filename.
Example: `torah-hebraic/the-machine-cannot-pray.md`

The `<articles-root>` is the working folder while drafting and the CDN once published, at `/articles` on the CDN origin. The five category folders are `covenant-identity`, `teshuvah-restoration`, `shalom-salvation`, `celebration-mishpakhah`, and `torah-hebraic`.

> Note the folder slugs are shorter than the reader-facing category names in Section 3, and one differs from its route: the site serves `torah-hebraic` at `/torah-hebraic-insights`. Use the folder names above for files, and the exact ampersand names from Section 3 for the `category` frontmatter field.

**The manifest is the source of truth.** `articles.json` lists what is published in that category. An article whose `.md` file exists but which is absent from the manifest does not appear on the site, and a category whose manifest carries `"articles": []` renders empty regardless of stray files beside it. Each entry mirrors the article's own frontmatter:

```json
{
  "category": "Torah & Hebraic Insights",
  "category_slug": "torah-hebraic",
  "office": "Teaching",
  "updated": "2026-07-30",
  "counts": { "total": 24, "generated": 12, "pending": 12 },
  "articles": [
    {
      "slug": "the-machine-cannot-pray",
      "title": "The Machine Cannot Pray",
      "file": "the-machine-cannot-pray.md",
      "author": "Zev Inspire",
      "office": "Teaching",
      "date_updated": "2026-07-30",
      "velocity_tier": "Rapid",
      "status": "published",
      "image_prompt": "Full prompt text here, ending with 16:9",
      "image_file": "",
      "image_status": "pending"
    }
  ]
}
```

**Do not hand-edit a manifest, and never write one from an article-authoring pass.** Rebuild it from the `.md` files instead:

```sh
node server/scripts/rebuild-article-manifests.js          # dry run
node server/scripts/rebuild-article-manifests.js --write  # apply
```

The rebuild derives every entry from frontmatter and derives `image_file` / `image_status` from what is actually on disk, so it is idempotent and self-healing: run it again after the image pipeline finishes and `pending` entries flip to `generated` on their own. It is also the reason a batch of authors can write concurrently. Twelve writers each touching five shared manifests would race and corrupt them; writers touch only their own `.md` files, and the manifest is rebuilt once at the end.

**Frontmatter (YAML, required):**

```yaml
---
title: "The Machine Cannot Pray"
slug: "the-machine-cannot-pray"
category: "Torah and Hebraic Insights"
office: "Teaching"

date_updated: "2026-07-30"

velocity_tier: "Rapid"
refresh_due: "2026-08-20"
refresh_note: ""
scripture_anchor: "John 3:16"

hook_question: "The single question that keeps the reader moving to the end."
reversal: "One line naming the assumption that flips, for QA use."

```

**Body skeleton:**

```markdown
# {Title}

*by {Author}*

{Cold open: 2 to 3 sentences on a real current event. No heading.}

## {Warm narrative heading, never the word "Parable"}

{Parable, part one. Ends at the Act II turn, unresolved.}

## {Teaching heading that raises a question or promises a discovery}

{The teaching. Returns to the characters by name at least twice, raising stakes each time.}

### {Sub-heading for the Hebrew or Paleo-Hebrew reveal}

{Word study delivered as a twist, explained plainly.}

## {Heading that signals the story is coming back}

{Parable resolution.}

## {Warm invitational heading, never "Call to Action"}

{Prayer, declaration, and/or action. Final line closes one question and opens one thread.}
```

Additional formatting rules:
- Scripture quotations use markdown blockquotes, with the reference and JSV on the line beneath.
- Headings are H2 for the movements, H3 for subsections. Never use H1 for anything except the title.
- Bold sparingly. No underlining. No all-caps.

## 17. House Style

- **Never use the em dash (—) anywhere, in any language or format.** Restructure the sentence, or use a comma, semicolon, colon, parentheses, or a period.
- Name the five-fold office **Shepherd**, never Pastor.
- Any rating or score is expressed as a percentage, 0 to 100%. Never stars, never out of ten, never letter grades.
- Interpretive material is an **interpretation**, never a "reading."
- Background processes are **services**, never daemons.

## 18. Pre-Delivery Checklist

Score each article before delivery. A composite below 85% goes back for revision.

| # | Check | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Cold open lands on a real current event in 2 to 3 sentences, no preaching | 5% |
| 2 | Parable is split and cuts at the Act II turn, unresolved | 10% |
| 3 | Three questions planted in the first 200 words, all accounted for | 5% |
| 4 | One face, exact details, and a ticking clock present | 10% |
| 5 | Teaching delivers something most believers do not know | 15% |
| 6 | Hebrew or Paleo-Hebrew delivered as a reveal and explained plainly | 10% |
| 7 | At least one true reversal, and it matches the title's paradox | 10% |
| 8 | Story returns twice inside the teaching, raising stakes each time | 5% |
| 9 | Resolution and invitation land together, with hope | 10% |
| 10 | Scroll velocity: phone-sized paragraphs, hooked section endings | 5% |
| 11 | Reading level verified at 8th grade, word count 2,000 to 4,500 | 5% |
| 12 | Frontmatter complete, image prompt written, 16:9 specified | 10% |

**Automatic fail conditions:**
- Any em dash in the output.
- A loop opened and never closed, or a promised revelation that turns out to be a familiar point.
- The parable resolved before the teaching begins.
- Internal mode labels or system explanations visible to the reader.
- "The Ruach HaKodesh" or any other doubled `Ha-` article.
- The word "Pastor" used as the name of the office.
- The phrase "Call to Action" printed in the article.
- A real named victim or real family attached to a fictional parable character.
- A negative, fearful, or condemning close.
- An `image_file` naming an image that has not actually been rendered. See Section 15: this removes the article from the generator's queue silently and permanently.

## 19. What This Site Is Not

- Not a devotional blog.
- Not a sermon transcript.
- Not a doom feed with Scripture attached.
- Not a place where familiar verses get their familiar explanations.
- Not a place that manufactures suspense it cannot pay off.

It is Good News, written for the person scrolling right now, carrying something ancient they have never been shown, told in a way they cannot walk away from.
